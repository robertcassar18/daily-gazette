Maltese Local News

Comino Hotel Project Moves Forward Amidst Fierce Opposition

The Environment and Resources Authority (ERA) has given the green light for preliminary environmental works on the contentious Six Senses Comino development, including the uprooting of approximately 800 trees. This decision comes despite ongoing legal challenges to the wider project, igniting strong condemnation from environmental non-governmental organizations.

Environmental NGOs, including Moviment Graffitti and Momentum, have vehemently criticized ERA's approval, arguing that allowing works to proceed before the conclusion of legal proceedings could lead to irreversible environmental damage. They highlight the removal of hundreds of trees from Comino's nature reserve and increased construction traffic. Momentum stated that the decision sends a message that environmental protection is secondary to development interests.

The developer, HV Hospitality, maintains that the approval followed a detailed review process and public consultation. They stated that around 550 native and protected trees and shrubs would be transplanted, with only about 50 permanently removed, each compensated at a ratio of 1:10. The company also pledged to introduce approximately 55,000 new trees and plants and restore over 8,200 square meters of previously developed land to its natural state.

The controversy underscores a broader public debate in Malta regarding overdevelopment and the preservation of green spaces, with residents across the islands protesting against similar destructions.

Malta's Economy Shows Robust Growth Amidst Inflation Concerns

Malta's economy expanded by 3.9% in the first quarter of 2026, a robust pace driven primarily by the services sector, outperforming the Euro area average. However, this growth rate is slightly lower than the previous year. Residential property prices continued their upward trend, increasing by 6.7% annually, fueled by strong tourism demand and migrant worker influx. While annual inflation, as measured by the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP), slowed to 2.3% in March 2026, underlying inflation remains a concern, standing above the Euro area average. The Central Bank of Malta's third Quarterly Review for 2026 highlighted a tight labor market with historically high job vacancies.

Second Sicily Interconnector Nears Completion, Boosting Energy Security

Malta has achieved a significant milestone in the construction of its second electricity interconnector with Sicily. Key equipment, including auto transformers and shunt reactors, has arrived and is being installed on both sides of the Mediterranean. This €300 million project, supported by €261 million in EU funding, is set to double Malta's electricity connection to the European grid. Once completed, the 122-kilometer interconnector between Magħtab and Ragusa will enhance the country's energy security and facilitate the integration of more renewable energy sources, such as wind and solar power. Energy Minister Miriam Dalli hailed this as another important step for the project.

Malta Developers Association Calls for Long-Term Energy Vision

Amid ongoing discussions about Malta's energy infrastructure, the Malta Developers Association (MDA) has proposed the establishment of a National Infrastructure and Energy Advisory Council. The MDA stressed that the country needs to build on its existing long-term energy vision through sustained investment rather than introducing a completely new strategy. Their proposals include further investment in the electricity distribution network, substations, transformers, underground cabling, and increased investment in energy storage systems, photovoltaic installations, and smart grid technologies. This comes after 2026 has seen the "worst year ever recorded for blackouts" in Malta, attributed to "shortcomings in planning and investment relative to rising demand."

European and International News

EU Strengthens Satellite Constellation IRIS² for Enhanced Security

On August 7, 2026, the European Commission and the SpaceRISE consortium finalized an implementation agreement to accelerate the rollout of IRIS², the European Union's new flagship satellite constellation. The agreement significantly expands the program by adding 66 satellites into low Earth orbit, bringing the main constellation to a total of 348 satellites. This reinforcement is aimed at strengthening capabilities for defense, security, and emergency services, increasing secure governmental capacity by 60% within the EU and 54% globally. The move transitions the project from planning to full-scale deployment with the support of the European Space Agency (ESA).

A World Bank Group report, "World Development Report 2026: The Promise of Artificial Intelligence," released on August 4, 2026, suggests that AI could enable developing countries to achieve in a decade what might otherwise take a century. This is contingent on governments swiftly addressing gaps in power, connectivity, skills, and institutional quality. ESA Prepares for August 12 Total Solar Eclipse Science Campaigns

The European Space Agency (ESA) is gearing up for extensive science campaigns during the total solar eclipse on August 12, 2026, which will be visible across parts of Europe. Plans include a joint ESA/Irish Air Corps C295 flight to chase the Moon's shadow, carrying an instrument from ESA's Proba-3 mission. Researchers are also utilizing advanced computer simulations and solar observations to predict the appearance of the Sun's corona during the eclipse, aiming to improve models for understanding the Sun's magnetic environment and forecasting space weather. ESA will broadcast the event live, allowing global audiences to experience this rare astronomical phenomenon.
